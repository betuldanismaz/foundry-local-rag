The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and the fifth largest moon in
the Solar System. It is thought to have formed about 4.5 billion years
ago, likely from debris left over after a Mars-sized object collided
with the early Earth.

The Moon is tidally locked to Earth, meaning the same side always faces
us — the "far side" of the Moon is never visible from Earth. It has no
atmosphere, so its surface experiences extreme temperature swings and is
covered in impact craters that remain undisturbed by weather. The Moon's
gravitational pull is the primary cause of Earth's ocean tides.
